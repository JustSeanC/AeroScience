# AeroScience v0.9.0 — Expanded Airborne Science & Environmental Missions

**Planning status:** Owner-directed target scope, recorded 2026-10-23. **Not implemented, scheduled, feasibility-accepted, or frozen for release.** This is a high-level planning baseline on a research-only branch; v0.7.0 remains research and v0.6.0 is separately in development. No v0.9.0 feature branch, merge, tag, build or deployment is authorized by this document.

## Product intent

Expand AeroScience beyond its existing instrument set and mission templates with **airborne meteorology, severe-weather reconnaissance, scientific imaging, environmental and ocean science, and radar research**. These should feel like coherent flight-based research missions, not disconnected overlays or arbitrary checklists. Keep local flight recording and independent science sessions. All scientific channels must disclose what is actually MSFS-reported, what is derived or simulated by an explicit model, and what comes from a dated external dataset or user import. Do not portray a fictional instrument as a certified real sensor or historic satellite imagery as a new airborne camera capture.

**v0.7.0 is to prepare the architecture only, not ship these specialized mission suites.** See [v0.7.0 future science architecture](v0.7.0-future-science-architecture.md) and [v0.7.0 technical research](v0.7.0-technical-research.md). A v0.8.0 implementation theme has not been established by this document; do not infer it or bind dependencies to an unapproved release.

## Owner-approved v0.9.0 planned mission families

| Group | Planned capabilities | Key feasibility / scientific constraints |
|---|---|---|
| WX-01 — Weather reconnaissance | Dedicated reconnaissance flights, repeated passes, atmospheric mapping and analysis; mission briefs and patterns for meteorological objectives | Only claim weather variables actually available at the aircraft or through documented weather/model sources; explicit temporal/spatial resolution |
| WX-02 — Simulated dropsondes & probes | Plan/release simulated sondes and atmospheric probes; event logs, descent trajectories, per-sample readings, simulated sensor models and recovered vertical profiles | Probe needs independently sourced/modelled environmental fields *at each probe position and time*. Aircraft's own ambient value is not automatically a descending sonde measurement. Need descent/drag model, spatial sampling validity, and unsupported state |
| WX-03 — Atmospheric profiles | Temperature, pressure, humidity, wind; vertical profiles and multi-altitude sampling | Humidity/wind/pressure/temperature availability and provenance may vary; no fabricated samples, proper altitudes/datums, units and QA |
| WX-04 — Vertical atmospheric sampling | Profile legs, climbs/descents, sampling plans, comparisons and temporal/geographic alignment | Separate flight-level repeated profiles from independently descending sondes; handle gaps, rate, time and altitude source |
| WX-05 — Turbulence & atmospheric structures | Observations and profile/transect analysis of turbulence-related variables and atmospheric structure | Do not claim direct turbulence intensity/eddy dissipation rate or cloud microphysics unless supported by trustworthy simulator variables or explicit models with limitations |
| STORM-01 — Hurricane reconnaissance | Storm-centered research missions and structured observation objectives | Simulator weather fidelity, real datasets, time/position reference and model provenance must be explicit |
| STORM-02 — Storm-relative patterns | Storm-centered survey patterns, e.g. radials, figure-four, eyewall approaches/crossings where supported | Moving center and time/reference uncertainties; navigation/simulation only, not real-world storm penetration or safety instructions |
| STORM-03 — Eyewall studies | Eyewall penetration and observation missions, structured conditions, observations and planned passes | No claim of physically accurate 3D simulated eyewall without validated supporting source/model |
| STORM-04 — Probe deployment | Simulated dropsonde planning, deployment, profile mapping and comparison across storm quadrants | Reuse WX probe models and distinct release/trajectory products; avoid second sonde engine |
| STORM-05 — Structure & intensity | Storm structure and intensity assessment across measurements, mapped positions and model/source-time context | Historical NOAA observations vs simulator weather must remain separately identified; no operational weather forecasts or current storm intensity claims without appropriate data |
| STORM-06 — Multi-aircraft campaigns | Offline-first coordinated research plans, assignments, compatible observation merges and cross-flight analysis | Multiple flights/users may contribute after explicit submissions; **no requirement to implement live multi-aircraft streaming in v0.9.0** unless separately authorized |
| IMG-01 — Simulated airborne cameras | Simulated aerial cameras and photographic survey missions, image/footprint metadata, sensor geometry, overlap and georeferencing where feasible | Investigate actual MSFS visual-frame capture rights/API/limitations and reliability; otherwise use clearly labeled modeled/derived products. Existing historical external imagery is not an onboard capture |
| FIRE-01 — Wildfire research | Research/survey patterns, mapped burn/smoke/thermal observations, repeated assessments | Must not invent physically accurate wildfire spread, plume chemistry, thermal intensity, or smoke concentration. Integrate authorized external data/models with dates and quality |
| OCEAN-01 — Ocean observation | Coastal/offshore surveys, sea-surface/temperature/conditions, environmental transects, georeferenced products | Source availability, units, vertical datum, ocean-surface models, spatial/temporal coverage and platform capabilities |
| ENV-01 — Environmental monitoring | Pollution/land-cover/ecosystem observations, repeat regional sampling, environmental changes and mission analysis | Distinguish real historical remote-sensing data and model-derived estimates from simulated onboard readings; avoid false high-resolution change conclusions |
| RADAR-01 — Airborne radar research | Research-inspired radar surveys/products; potential mapping, reflectivity/volume/range-time or other supported classes | Determine actual supported data source and feasible validated radar model; no fabricated returns or implied real onboard Doppler/radar capability |

All listed topics belong to the **v0.9.0 planned scope inventory**, not a promise of particular instrument fidelity or fully implemented release features. Detailed prioritization, dependency ordering, and acceptance scope will be established in v0.9.0 research and owner review. If simulation/model/data-access feasibility blocks a capability, keep its scope visible and document the limitation and needed research rather than quietly mislabeling it as complete.

## Shared design: missions, data products and scientific evaluation

- Reuse existing Universal/Realistic science modes. Science sessions remain separate from flight recording; one sortie may fulfill multiple independently evaluated objectives and scientific campaigns.
- Reuse the existing ScienceCatalog/InstrumentProfiles/ResearchOperations/ScienceStore/external dataset acquisition systems where compatible. Do not build a parallel telemetry recorder for each mission suite.
- Capability-based sensors with stable IDs, typed channel declarations, validity envelopes, source and model revision/hash, units, uncertainty, geometry, time, quality and documented gaps.
- Product families must cover time-series numeric samples; spatial trajectories and vertical profiles; geo-tagged image frames/georeferenced rasters; swaths and grids; optional radar-style range/volume products; events/deployments; annotations/review evidence. Avoid a schema that assumes one scalar measurement per one-second flight packet.
- Physical modeling/source research first: probe trajectory + independently sampled atmosphere, storm-center representation, MSFS camera-frame capture, ocean/fire/environment data availability, and radar signal/return feasibility. No ungrounded simulated measurements.
- Spatial and temporal semantics: aircraft/probe positions and altitude datum; WGS84 and appropriate CRS; source original observation time vs in-sim flight timestamp vs modeled processing; source uncertainty and sampling geometry.
- Explicit supported/unsupported states. Models must be repeatable from frozen configuration and recorded inputs where possible. Clearly label simulated versus historical observations.
- Store large imagery, profiles and grids separately from compact mission/event rows; streamed/bounded processing, region/time selectors, viewport pagination, user-selected storage and accurate space estimates.
- Scientific library reuse: reuse compatible local terrain and sources whenever justified; no arbitrary overall collection-count ceiling as a substitute for storage limits.
- Permanent deletion: removal of user-selected scientific/external data includes managed originals and regenerable copies with no secret local backups/caches/archives. Independently retained mission results survive only if self-contained; otherwise show missing dependencies. A separately submitted organization copy has its own disclosed retention policy.
- Organization/evidence integration: capability-based test cards, independently evaluated multiple objectives per flight, compact manifest references and deliberate consent for larger binaries or sensor products; no automatic source-library upload.
- Keep live Mission Control (streamed aircraft position/instrument data and real-time direction) deferred and separate. Multi-aircraft science can work by coordinating plans and merging **postflight submissions**.

## Architecture obligations for v0.7.0

These are **compatibility design requirements**, not an instruction to build v0.9.0 instruments early:

1. Use a versioned `ScientificCapabilityDescriptor`/equivalent describing source channel, measured vs modeled vs imported origin, valid units/quality, spatial geometry and supported product shapes, with old records readable.
2. Allow `ScientificProductReference`/equivalent to point at large immutable products by stable ID/source hash, not embed image/volume binaries in test cards or primary flight tables.
3. Model time-bounded source relationships: independently moving atmospheric probe, aircraft timestamp and original external observation date. Never assume probe data is just aircraft telemetry.
4. Permit research cards/objectives to consume typed scalar data, profile series, spatial image/coverage summaries, event/deployment records, environmental grids or derived science, with explicit unsupported/future capability states.
5. Represent optional relative reference frames (e.g. storm-relative) and extensible flight patterns without hardcoding hurricanes in current generic mission DTOs.
6. Support cross-mission/postflight scientific result aggregation and organization submissions with independent approvals, provenance and no mandatory live service.
7. Preserve local first, user-controlled storage and deletion, provenance and scientific integrity, and zero unsolicited live telemetry. Reuse existing instrument/data-library infrastructure and do not add an arbitrary executable plugin engine for future-proofing.
