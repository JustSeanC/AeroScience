# AeroScience v0.7.0 — Product Design Decisions (Round 1–2)

**Captured 2026-10-23.** User-directed product design; not equivalent to implemented code or production acceptance. Maintained on a research-only branch, independent of develop/v0.6.0.

## Confirmed from owner interview — Round 1

1. **Organization feel:** hybrid professional research/test operations and approachable collaborative science. Neither an intimidating formal-only platform nor generic social/task management.
2. **Interfaces:** **both** the standalone organization web portal and integrated existing AeroScience desktop dashboard, working together. Administrators/managers use the portal; pilots should be able to handle assignment, science preparation, flight and submissions from the desktop. Avoid duplicate unsynchronized state.
3. **Campaign authoring:** templates first. Prioritize concise guided setup and customization rather than a mandatory blank-canvas authoring system.
4. **Pilot workflows:** both guided assignment-to-flight setup and independent manual mission planning.
5. **Review:** campaign-configurable automated checks, human review or hybrid; evaluation statuses remain evidence-based and distinguish simulator observations from real-world scientific validation.

## Confirmed from owner interview — Round 2

1. **Membership:** each organization chooses invitation-only or discoverable/open application enrollment, with organization-controlled approval policy. Public discovery does not grant access by default.
2. **Assignments:** manager assignment and volunteer/self-claim supported.
3. **Collaboration:** individual assigned flights as well as coordinated multi-pilot projects, shared program objectives and combined results.
4. **Live monitoring — superseded by owner revision:** the original Round 2 answer favored detailed live maps and instrument monitoring, but the **later decision explicitly defers live organization operations beyond v0.7.0**. **v0.7.0 ships local science/flight-test recording, user-controlled evidence submission and organization review/status; no live flight map, active telemetry streaming, watch room, or real-time science monitoring.** Lay out minimal future-ready, inert backend extension contracts and security boundaries only; do not activate data flow, background uploads, live endpoints, telemetry retention or monitoring user interfaces. The original preference is retained here for historical clarity, not as v0.7.0 scope.

## New owner vision: science AND experimental flight testing

AeroScience v0.7.0 must be suitable for both:
- Multi-aircraft airborne science campaigns (atmosphere, surveying, collection and analysis).
- Aircraft/systems/software/hardware validation programs resembling **Boom XB-1** or **NASA X-plane** development flight-test campaigns: mission parameters/test points, envelope/test-phase progression, aircraft/configuration-specific evaluations, repeat attempts and reviewer decisions.

**Do not force users into a binary application operating mode.** Research and developmental test campaigns should share the core program/assignment/evidence backbone and use appropriate templates and measurable criteria. A manager may combine collection science and development test objectives in one broader program.

Suggested hierarchy for exploration (not yet owner-approved):
Organization > Program > Phase/Campaign > Flight Assignment > Test Card > Test Point > Evidence/Evaluation/Review. **Round 4 resolved:** a single recorded sortie can contain multiple test cards, each independently evaluated; determine whether overlapping measurements may qualify for more than one card under explicit policy. Distinguish pass, fail, insufficient evidence, not attempted, deferred, and awaiting review. Reflight/retest should preserve historical attempts.

Real test context for future reference:
- XB-1 official flight test blog records individual flight numbers, max speed/altitude, and specific test points (e.g., cockpit pressurization, augmentation off, flutter excitation, Mach targets): https://boomsupersonic.com/flyby/xb-1-live-blog-flight-test-program
- NASA's X-59 describes progressive envelope expansion, test-card execution and data review between flights: https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/armstrong/nasas-x-59-prepares-for-second-flight/ and https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/quesst/2026/09/04/nasas-x-59-aircraft-builds-momentum-through-25th-flight/
- NASA flight-test handbook describes test-card fields: aircraft ID, revision, objective, aircraft/test configuration, maneuver and acceptance criteria: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20140010192

**Scientific integrity:** use typed MSFS telemetry/recorded observations only for values truly exposed; structural loads/flutter margin, avionics internals, simulated hardware status, configuration build identity and software validity may be unavailable. Allow an explicit Unsupported / Manually Attested / Externally Imported distinction and never certify real-world aircraft safety. A campaign clearance/unlock is an in-app coordination status, not real airworthiness authorization.

## Latest scope decision — owner revision

**2026-10-23:** Keep v0.7.0 **local collection + explicit postflight submission**. Build a foundation for a future live-monitoring backend, but do not ship the live feature now. Pilot assignments and mission/result status can synchronize as discrete actions; this does not authorize continuous telemetry. A potential future live path needs a separate opt-in consent model, organization-scoped auth, stream schemas, throttling, retention limits, and failure isolation. Reserving versioned extension points and testing their inactivity is preferable to creating an unused operational streaming stack. This decision overrides Round 2 item 4 above.


## Owner design decisions — Round 3 (2026-10-23)

All five selections are **product-direction decisions**, with details to design/test; no feature implementation is yet approved.

1. **Campaign complexity: flexible.** A straightforward research campaign should remain simple; developmental flight-test programs may opt into a hierarchy such as program > phase > assignment > test card > test point > attempt/evidence/review. Avoid forcing casual research campaigns through experimental aircraft phase gates.
2. **Test-point completion: hybrid.** Where supported and recorded, evaluate quantitative criteria (values, units, tolerances, quality, sustained duration and explicit missing-data rules) automatically. Where simulator variables are unavailable or the objective is observational, collect human observations and provenance; review independently. No implied flightworthiness certification or fabricated simulated avionics data.
3. **Flight-envelope phase clearances: configurable.** A manager can require review/approval of prior phase objectives before later cards are authorized *within the organization workflow*. No control or restriction of the simulator, aircraft, recorder, or independent local science activity. Gate policy versions and approvals must be auditable; bypass/waivers are proposed research topics, not yet accepted.
4. **Primary test scope — clarified after Round 4:** the Round 3 wording about MSFS aircraft add-on validation does **not** mean campaigns are centered on certifying a specific new aircraft or add-on. Campaigns should be **mostly aircraft agnostic** and primarily test fictional/experimental simulated scientific payloads, hardware sensors, avionics/software/system concepts in defined flight regimes on **any eligible MSFS aircraft**. Example: fictional new hardware sensor carried by a fast jet, validated at specified Mach/altitude/temperature/operating conditions. Aircraft/add-on identity is recorded for reproducibility; required flight regime, instrument/sensor capability and reliable data are the eligibility basis. Actual hardware certification and external physical hardware testing are not release goals.
5. **Live Mission Control concept: eventual full test-director workflow.** Desired future experience includes monitoring, coordination, managing test points, anomaly recording, and recommending holds or aborts **without taking aircraft control**. **Not a v0.7.0 live feature.** The owner's later local-collection + explicit-submission scope is authoritative. v0.7.0 can support non-live anomaly reports, review notes, assignments, phases and hold recommendations as asynchronous records; streaming/real-time interaction remains for a separately approved future release.

### Practical implications and unconfirmed proposals

- One unified campaign/evidence core serves science and experimental flight testing; optional templates expose more advanced program components. Avoid introducing another global flight/science operating mode.
- Track test point state distinctly from whole-flight completion: NotAttempted, Recorded/PendingEvaluation, Pass, Fail, InsufficientEvidence, NeedsReview, Deferred/Exception (names illustrative). Preserve each attempt and its independent evidence, policy revision, source provenance and evaluation method.
- Quantitative checks need explicit continuous hold/window semantics, cadence/gaps, units/quality, and whether manually supplied values count; prohibit labeling pilot-reported or unavailable avionics as simulator-verified.
- Configurable milestones represent **administrative project progression**, not real airworthiness clearance. Include pause/hold advisory records only as human communications, never simulator-control commands.
- **Round 4 answered:** one sortie may fulfill several test cards, with independent eligibility, attempt history, evidence range and evaluation for each card. Determine how to assign/start/stop each card, how to prevent incompatible test requirements, and whether explicitly shared telemetry intervals can be evaluated by multiple cards.
- Clarify re-attempt behavior, whether retests replace or append, and exact manual-review gates.

## Remaining design questions

- Program and campaign hierarchy; stage-gates and progression.
- Test-card detail depth, automatic vs observational/manual success criteria.
- How to identify aircraft model, tail, installed add-ons and software/hardware revisions without claiming simulator evidence.
- **Deferred beyond v0.7.0:** live telemetry scope, explicit consent, monitoring roles, stream retention/replay, update budget and live UI. For v0.7.0 research, define optional capability negotiation, event envelope/provenance and a separately permissioned transport interface without shipping a working streaming service.
- Team/chase aircraft assignments, coordinated time windows and combined evidence.
- Templates catalog and who can publish reusable/custom org templates.
- Failure, retry, partial completion, waiver and human authority models.
- Organization identity/branding, self-host usability and measured cost.

## Development boundary

No feature implementation or main/develop branch modification authorized by this document. Keep v0.6.0 priorities and scientific data fixes separate; do not repurpose local paired-device authentication for remote users.

## Owner Round 4 — multi-card sorties and aircraft-agnostic simulated test campaigns (2026-10-23)

**Decisions:** (1) **Multiple test cards per flight**, each independently evaluated. (2) Campaigns are **mostly aircraft agnostic**, using compatible aircraft as flying platforms for fictional experimental research payloads and simulated hardware/software/sensor validation within defined flight regimes; do not presume an actual new-aircraft test/certification program. These decisions take precedence over earlier F-15/XB-1 examples when they imply aircraft-specific scope. XB-1 is an **inspiration for the test-card/process structure**, not the required subject of a campaign.

**Illustrative scenario:** a manager publishes a fictional experimental sensor that must be evaluated across low-speed baseline, transonic and higher-altitude operating regimes. Any fast jet that can actually achieve applicable constraints may participate; selection follows observed performance/capability and explicitly configured requirements, **not an immutable aircraft whitelist**. A single sortie could complete multiple cards. One completed card must not automatically pass another; each uses its own criteria, windows, instrument/source provenance, and evidence/evaluation snapshots.

**Proposed data boundaries:**
- `Flight` has identity, aircraft description/unknowns, start/end, immutable telemetry/evidence archive; holds zero or more `CardAttempt`s.
- Each `CardAttempt` references exactly one immutable test-card revision plus its own start/end evidence selection (temporal windows, measurement IDs, session IDs, provenance), state, evaluation and reviews. Many cards can reference the same sortie without copying flight samples.
- A card specifies required **measurable flight regime** (Mach/IAS and correctly distinguished airspeed types, altitude reference, condition duration/cadence/tolerances, environmental limits), payload model/compatible instrument channels, and any user-declared aircraft capability. Aircraft make/model may be informative, but must not imply a measurement is available.
- A fictional sensor must declare its data mode: **modeled/derived from explicitly identified simulator signals**, user-observed/manual evidence, or externally supplied supported data. If neither evidence source nor scientific simulation model exists, result is `Unsupported` or `InsufficientEvidence`, never fabricated sensor performance.
- Cache mission/science acquisition once; analyze each card against its own criteria. Historic attempts remain immutable after card edits. One test card can be retried during a later flight, which creates a new attempt.

**Technical research tasks (not yet accepted implementations):** temporal mapping of multiple cards to one flight, duplicate-proof sample references, independent quality/gap handling per card, sustained-regime verification, conflicts between mutually exclusive tests, aircraft capability checks based on real telemetry, modeled sensor provenance, and privacy-aware submission package selecting only the evidence each card needs. Preserve local-only acquisition and postflight opt-in submission; no live streaming.

## Owner Round 5 — hybrid test-condition and modeled-sensor experiments (2026-10-23)

**Confirmed:** v0.7.0 campaign test cards may be either (a) **flight-condition-only tests**, which evaluate recorded flight-regime evidence such as Mach, altitude, temperature, cadence or stable duration and optionally collect human observations; or (b) **modeled experimental-sensor tests**, which use explicitly documented, reproducible fictional instrument simulation built from identified simulator measurements and/or authorized scientific datasets. An overall campaign or sortie may contain both categories, with each card independently evaluated.

**Scope and integrity constraints:**
- No assumption that a fictional sensor's actual hardware exists in MSFS, that MSFS provides hidden avionics/structural variables, or that generated readings are real measurements. Explicitly label simulator-reported inputs vs simulated/model-derived sensor channels vs external historical scenes vs manual evidence.
- Version/freeze the effective modeled sensor definition (model ID/version/hash, inputs, units, settings, calibration assumptions where relevant, noise/latency/range rules, limitations, seed if stochastic) with each attempt so historical results remain reproducible. Reject or mark unsupported when inputs are unavailable rather than fabricate successful results.
- Prefer reusing AeroScience's existing instrument/profile/evidence/model architecture rather than a second independent instrument engine. **Selected after this round:** managers configure supported experimental-sensor templates; arbitrary model-code authoring is not approved for v0.7.0.
- Flight recording and scientific collection remain local. Pilot preview and explicit postflight submission are still the only organizational evidence-transfer path in v0.7.0; live monitoring remains deferred.

**Illustrative mixed sortie:** One eligible jet flight captures a sustained transonic interval for condition-only Card A and runs a separately labeled *simulated* experimental atmospheric-sensor response against available modeled atmospheric inputs for Card B. Both link to the same flight but are evaluated separately, and either may remain insufficient-evidence.

## Round 5 follow-up — configurable sensors and optional aircraft test subject (2026-10-23)

**Owner decision:** choose **Configurable Experimental Sensors** for v0.7.0: a manager starts from a supported scientific sensor/instrument model template, adjusts documented parameters (e.g. operating range, sensitivity, response time, latency, modeled noise/uncertainty, applicability and sampling characteristics), previews expected behavior, and publishes a versioned immutable definition for card evaluation. No user-authored executable code, unrestricted custom sensor algorithms or arbitrary scripts are approved. Use existing AeroScience science/payload/calibration conventions; any unsupported parameter is explicit rather than faked. Models are clearly labeled simulations derived from declared inputs.

**Owner clarification:** In addition to mostly aircraft-agnostic scientific research and experimental sensor validation, v0.7.0 should **also** support creating **fictional experimental aircraft validation programs/cards** inspired by X-plane/XB-1-style campaigns. These are legitimate campaign subjects, not just sensor-related test conditions. Examples include speed/altitude/flight-envelope verification, handling assessment, simulated avionics/systems behavior, repeatability and phase progression. A campaign template may identify its test **subject** as scientific collection, simulated payload/system, aircraft/flight-test, or a mixture; share the same mission, test-point, evaluation and explicit submission infrastructure.

**Nuance:** **Most campaigns remain aircraft agnostic by default.** A fictional aircraft-validation program may reasonably refer to a particular fictional/installed aircraft or a class of compatible aircraft, but **the manager may optionally impose a specific aircraft type, class, or configuration**, when defensible for the experiment; this was confirmed in the subsequent aircraft eligibility decision. Do not turn an XB-1 process example into a mandatory real-aircraft certification workflow. No real-world certification, hardware safety assurance or unsupported flight-dynamics measurement claims.

**Integrity boundary:** Automatically evaluate aircraft-test criteria only from valid simulator-recorded flight conditions and supported exposed variables. Handling qualities, control-law internals, flutter, structural margins and fictional avionics state cannot be presented as measured unless a genuine supported signal or documented modeled channel exists; permit explicitly labeled pilot/engineer observations. Keep local acquisition and intentional postflight submission; no live organizational streaming in v0.7.0.

## Aircraft eligibility decision — flexible restrictions (2026-10-23)

**Owner confirmed:** Aircraft eligibility is **configurable**. The normal/default setting accepts any suitable aircraft; managers may instead require an **aircraft class/capability** or **specified aircraft type/configuration** when the actual fictional experimental aircraft validation or sensor mission demands it. This is an administrative criterion for a test card/program, not control of MSFS aircraft selection, flight or flight recording.

**Recommended eligibility structure (proposed detail):** `AnyCapableAircraft` (default), `CapabilityOrClassConstraints` (examples: eligible fast-jet/high-altitude platform, range capability, required channels, reported flight regime), or `ExactAircraftOrConfiguration` (aircraft identity / add-on package & version / simulated sensor or avionics configuration). Keep a declared matching policy and a reason for restrictions; allow campaign defaults with explicit card-level stricter overrides, subject to technical design review. A class label, model name or pilot declaration is **not** automatically sufficient scientific proof of measured capabilities.

**Evidence and integrity:** Record aircraft identity from MSFS when accessible (with source and uncertainty), source of add-on name/version/variant/configuration (simulator reported vs user declared vs reviewer attested), applicable environment/profile revision, and the actual flight conditions achieved. Distinguish `Eligible`, `NotEligible`, `Unverified/NeedsReview` so unknown aircraft IDs or externally configured add-on versions do not become invented verified matches. Evaluate each independently attempted card's flight-condition/sensor criteria separately from eligibility. If an exact aircraft is required, maintain an explicit manual review path where technical identification is unavailable rather than silently whitelisting the plane.

**Next interview topic:** manager-facing test-card builder — how authors define quantitative criteria, duration/repetition and windows, combine multiple conditions, identify evidence intervals, and request human observations. Local-only collection and explicit postflight submission remain unchanged, and the future live monitoring architecture is inactive in v0.7.0.

## Owner Round 6 — authoring, compound criteria, attempt discovery, retries (2026-10-23)

**Decisions confirmed by owner:**
1. **Test-card authoring:** Both guided creation and a more capable advanced editor. Both interfaces must represent the same versioned test-card schema; a complex card must never be silently flattened, destroyed or downgraded by opening the guided builder.
2. **Criteria:** Both standard and advanced conditions, including logical groups, ordered sequences, sustained windows, and repeated measurements or maneuvers. Examples: altitude AND Mach concurrently for 30 continuous seconds; advance to the next regime afterward; repeat a specific point three times. Authoring remains configuration-based (not arbitrary uploaded code).
3. **Attempt identification:** Both explicit pilot-marked attempts and automatic discovery of qualifying recorded intervals. Auto-detection should produce auditable candidates or interpreted attempts with explicit evidence bounds; manual markers use recorded times/source events and must not magically assert measurements. How the pilot confirms/discards automatic candidate intervals remains to design.
4. **Retries:** Multiple documented attempts in the same sortie and across sorties, with no fixed product-level number-of-attempts cap, independent attempt IDs and complete immutable history. Storage limits, cursor pagination, bounded processing and safe archiving still apply, without silently dropping failed attempts.

**Implications:**
- One flight can include many cards; one card can contain multiple test points, and each point may have multiple attempts. Do not conflate flight completed, attempt valid, test point passed, card approved and campaign phase cleared.
- Each attempt retains immutable card/revision/hash, flight + science-session IDs, explicit source time range or sample sequence, provenance, values/unit/quality, evaluation version, incomplete/missing data explanations, and user annotation/reviewer findings.
- Support boolean logical criteria, sequential dependencies and repeat counts, but evaluate documented source quality and gap semantics; a missing/invalid sampled interval must not count as a sustained continuous pass.
- Automatic interval detection must be deterministic and rerunnable on saved local flight data, not require live uploads, dashboard polling or a remote service. A pilot's explicit start/end is a meaningful evidence annotation and can mark otherwise unsupported observational maneuvers.
- Retakes never erase the first failure, and a later redefinition of a test card/sensor model may not silently rewrite earlier outcomes.
- Manager review policy remains configurable by campaign, with human assessment available for unsupported sensors/avionics and handling reports.
- All science and flight recordings remain local; pilots deliberately preview and submit selected evidence after flight. No real-time server telemetry in v0.7.0.

**Next interview topic:** Pilot-side test-card workflow (flight preparation, which cards are active, live *local* test hints during flight vs postflight evaluation, automatic-detection confirmation), plus per-card completion policy, required/optional attempt sharing, and submission UX. These remain open.

## Owner Round 7 — claims, local toolbar, automatic attempts, waivers and visibility (2026-10-23)

**Confirmed owner choices:**
1. **Active MSFS toolbar test-card interface:** show selected cards/objectives, quantitative requirements and live **local** progress, manual attempt controls and observational notes during flight. This is local simulator/companion display and must not imply live server monitoring or organizational telemetry upload. Maintain existing recording/science session independence and toolbar/CommBus size/interaction limits; UI must be usable in small panels.
2. **Automatic test-attempt identification:** locally detect and **record every distinct detected attempt** without requiring pilot confirmation; immediately evaluate based on eligible recorded data/modelled science where measurable, retaining fails/interruptions/insufficient-evidence as well as successes when evidence makes an attempt identifiable. Also support pilot-marked attempts; deduplicate overlaps deterministically and distinguish observed attempt from verified passed test. Submission remains user-confirmed *after* flight.
3. **Multi-pilot participation:** the manager can explicitly enable multiple pilots on a given card. **Normal cards are exclusively claimable by one pilot at a time.** Eligible pilots may freely browse and claim available unclaimed cards (unless assigned/reserved by a manager); a card's published multi-pilot policy permits multiple independent concurrent claims and independent attempts/reviews. Per-card pilot-count limits, release/expiry/reassignment policy and completion aggregation are *not yet decided*.
4. **Unsuccessful test points:** authorized managers may issue documented waivers or approve partial results. Never rewrite recorded FAIL/InsufficientEvidence into PASS. Keep immutable evidence/attempt outcomes plus distinct authorization/review outcome (Waived, ApprovedWithExceptions) and approver, reason, effective version and audit trail.
5. **Approved results visibility:** all organization members may browse approved research results. Unapproved drafts, private pilot archive, unrelated flight telemetry, original remote datasets and unpublished evidence are not thereby shared. Public visibility outside the organization was not approved.

**Owner's explicit allocation example (20 unique cards, 21 claims):**
- Pilot A claims test cards 1–10 and 15 (11 claims).
- Pilot B claims cards 11–13 (3 claims).
- Pilot C claims cards 14–20 (7 claims).
- Card 15 is configured by the manager as multi-pilot and appears in both Pilot A and Pilot C's assignments; both submit separately evaluated attempts. All other 19 cards have one exclusive claimant. The representation of card status must not assume one claimant per card; track claims and submissions as independent records.

**Architectural proposal / unresolved policy choices:** use transactionally atomic server claims and reject concurrent duplicate claims for default exclusive cards; only allow additional claims when multi-pilot flag/capacity permits. Claim lifecycle Available/Claimed/Released/Completed (exact display states subject to UX); never auto-release a pilot's work without explicit configurable rules and notice. Per-pilot assignment record belongs to a card/version, with independently immutable flight attempts, submissions and reviews. Manager assignment and self-claim share this one authoritative ownership model. Card completion should depend on configured multi-pilot evidence/review criteria; a single approved attempt does not necessarily satisfy required independent replicates.

**Next design questions:** for multi-pilot cards, how many independent claims/results are required for completion, and can extra pilots join? How do pilots release a card or managers reclaim stale cards? Does an approved partial result count toward campaign completion? Should toolbar show all claimed cards with manual focus/switching and automatically evaluate all relevant cards concurrently? What detailed evidence is visible with approved result summaries to other members?

## Owner Round 8 — card completion, release, activation and waivers (2026-10-23)

**Confirmed choices (supersede open questions left in Round 7):**
1. **Configurable multi-pilot thresholds:** Managers set the number of **distinct pilots** whose results are required, the maximum simultaneous/total participating pilots as defined in the UI, and the applicable review/approval requirements. A multi-pilot card is complete only when its defined required number of **independently accepted eligible pilot submissions** meets the configured review criteria, or has an explicitly authorized completion exception. Validate required <= maximum; independent repeated attempts by one person do not count as separate pilots. A card that is exclusively claimable uses one active claimant unless explicitly changed by authorized policy revision.
2. **Unconditional pilot release of claims:** Pilots can release claimed cards whenever they choose (including after attempts) without deleting their locally recorded attempts/science, earlier locally retained observations, or an already submitted server copy. The release makes an uncompleted exclusively claimable slot available to another eligible pilot; it is not permission to see the original pilot's private data. Persist the release and subsequent ownership change as auditable events. Historic accepted results and approval stay linked to the contributor and policy. Release and disconnect never alter the simulator's recording. Avoid silently losing unsubmitted evidence; inform pilot about how to submit past attempts or keep them locally. Manager reassign controls remain a separate policy decision.
3. **Explicit selection of simultaneously active cards in MSFS:** Pilots choose which claimed/assigned cards are **active for test monitoring**, and may enable **multiple active cards at once**, while a single focused card or a compact overview can be displayed in the toolbar. The automatic attempt detector evaluates/records the selected active cards in parallel from local recorded data. Claimed but inactive cards must not silently receive asserted success just by remaining claimed. Activation should not start/stop base flight recording or science acquisition and must not generate server telemetry. A later postflight search/backfill policy for inactive card data is *not yet decided*.
4. **Waiver/approved-partial counts for progress:** Authorized manager-approved documented waivers and partial results **can satisfy test-card/campaign completion criteria**, including applicable phase progression after recording the authorization. Never change underlying attempted measurement FAIL or InsufficientEvidence to Pass; display fulfilled-by-waiver/approved-exception distinctly from a measured pass. Retain actor, reason, scope, timestamp, evidence link and effective approval/policy version in audit records. Approved member-facing archive shows scientific outcome and the exceptional review state clearly.

**Compatibility with earlier choices:** Free self-claiming for eligible pilots; multi-pilot only when manager enables on card; 20 unique cards may have 21 independent pilot claims because #15 is multi-pilot. Automatic detection logs every distinct detected attempt for active cards without needing pilot confirmation, preserving failures/retries. Managers still set campaign approval approach (automatic/human/both); approved findings browsable by all organization members. Flight/science capture remains local with user-initiated selective postflight evidence submission. No live organizational position/instrument streaming in v0.7.0.

**Design follow-ups:** Definition of slot capacity for multi-pilot card (total historical contributors versus current active claims), whether a late pilot may join after minimum results met, whether a prior contributor who released a card can claim again, safe offline claim-release queue and conflict notices, toolbar performance under many simultaneous cards, and whether data from before activation may count via a deliberate after-flight re-evaluation. Clarify submission packaging for multiple cards in one recorded flight and whether approved result attachments are visible to all members.
