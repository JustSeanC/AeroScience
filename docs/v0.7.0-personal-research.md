# Personal Research — Local Experiments

Your experiments stay on your computer. You do not need an account or internet access.

## Scientific Observation Flight

Ask: **What can I describe and document during this flight?**

1. Open **My Research → Scientific Observation Flight → Fly Now**. **Customize** lets you change your own question, observation goal and optional instruments. No internet, downloaded data or organization is needed.
2. Click **Start mission**, then **Record Pilot Observation**. Choose a **Category**, write **Notes**, and click **Add observation marker**. **Save in-flight note** saves words without a location. In the native toolbar, open **Pilot Observation**, choose a category, write a short observation and click **Add observation marker**. Optional instruments need **Start new science session**; notes do not.
3. Click **End mission**, then **My Research → Review Scientific Observations → Evaluate Scientific Observations**. Look for your original words, times and available locations. **Inspect Observation** opens nearby recorded evidence. **Overview & Compare** compares repeated investigations. If something looks wrong, send a screenshot and say what you expected.

The default goal is three written records in the Observation, Finding or Note categories. A location marker without words does not meet that goal. **Collection Complete** means the documentation requirements were met. **Partial Collection** means useful documentation exists but requirements are missing. **Insufficient Evidence** means no usable documentation exists for the selected objective. These labels never prove that a written interpretation is true, and two notes are not automatically two independent scientific measurements.

Your original host UTC is recorded when you save; it is not an inferred simulator event time. A fresh recorded aircraft position may be attached to a marker. Missing locations stay unavailable, including while paused or disconnected, and are not filled from the flight path. You may require locations or every selected category in Customize. In-flight notes have no location; use a marker when that is needed.

Optional temperature, pressure, wind and density are separate original simulator readings with their own units and UTC. Near-note readings come from the preceding three seconds and do not prove what caused an observation. No reading is invented from a live display or raw flight context. If its Library is unavailable, the pilot documentation can still reopen while supporting values remain unverified. Preview tables/maps are bounded; the existing archive and timeline retain the original records.

Deleting collected science removes the associated Pilot Observations, research notes and dependent results, including registered app backups. Unrelated flight recordings and external source files remain separate. This optional three-step check can wait until your next Windows/MSFS flight; automated development tests do not claim installed or physical-device acceptance.

## Terrain & Temperature Survey

Ask: **What terrain and temperatures were sampled along my flight?**

1. Open **My Research → Terrain & Temperature Survey**. Keep **Air Temperature** for a flight needing no downloaded data, or select **Terrain Elevation** and/or **Land Surface Temperature**. Click **Fly Now**, or **Customize** to edit your own plan.
2. Click **Start mission**, then **Start new science session**. Fly to a few different places. For terrain or satellite temperature, **Review Selected Sources** and the normal Library/readiness controls explain what data is available. Missing data does not stop local air-temperature science.
3. Click **End mission**, then **My Research → Review Terrain & Temperature Survey → Evaluate Terrain & Temperature Survey**. Look for separate outcomes, units and sampled points for each measurement. Use **Overview & Compare** for repeated flights. If something looks wrong, send a screenshot and say what you expected.

The default needs three qualified samples at two locations at least 100 m apart for **each** selected measurement. **Collection Complete** means those collection goals were met; **Partial Collection** means some useful measurements exist; **Insufficient Evidence** means the selected goal has no qualified retained values. These are collection outcomes, not proof of a scientific hypothesis.

Terrain is DEM/DSM elevation in metres with its original vertical datum and grid resolution, **not airborne LiDAR**. Unknown datum stays unqualified; some USGS exports do not specify it. Landsat temperature is **historical satellite surface temperature in Kelvin**, with its original acquisition date and QA, **not an aircraft thermal camera**. Per-pixel uncertainty remains Unknown when the source did not retain it. Air temperature is simulator-reported Celsius at the aircraft. These are separate quantities.

Results show actual retained points, never a filled-in continuous scan. The flight line is context, not evidence of surrounding scientific coverage. Click **Inspect Saved Evidence** to follow a reading into the existing timeline. Datums/resolutions/source groups stay separate in comparisons. External-original deletion can leave independently saved observations available; deleting collected science removes dependent results and cannot regenerate them from the flight path. This optional three-step check can wait until your next Windows/MSFS flight; automated tests do not claim installed/device acceptance.

## Atmospheric Transect

Ask: **How do atmospheric conditions change as my aircraft travels across a region?**

1. Open **My Research → Atmospheric Transect → Fly Now**. **Customize** opens your own copy in Mission Planner. You can change the question, sampling locations and distance goals, or use the normal route tools. You can prepare free flight while disconnected.
2. Click **Start mission**, then **Start new science session**. Fly across different places in the simulator. Recording, science collection and card activation are separate. The default asks for three sampled locations at least 500 m apart, with three readings at each and 1,000 m extent in one supported leg.
3. Click **End mission**, then **My Research → Review Atmospheric Transect → Evaluate Atmospheric Transect**. Look for actual readings on the map and temperature chart. Click a sample or **Inspect Reading** to see its evidence. **Overview & Compare** lets you compare repeated flights. If something looks wrong, send a screenshot and say what you expected.

**Completed collection** means enough geographic science evidence was saved. **Partial coverage** means useful readings exist but the goals were not met. **Insufficient or unavailable evidence** means no valid located temperature science was retained. Stationary readings and repeat visits do not add new sampling locations. Pauses, time/position gaps and session changes break the sampled path; missing areas remain missing.

Distance includes supported links between recorded temperature samples, not unmeasured gaps. Lines illustrate sample order, not a continuous measured atmosphere. Altitude changes, elapsed time, weather and measurement settings can explain differences between places or flights. Pressure, wind and density appear only when supported original readings exist. Exact original coordinates remain in the table, including polar locations beyond the map's display range. Science deletion removes dependent transect results; raw flight recordings cannot recreate them.

These three optional app-only checks can wait until your next Windows/MSFS flight. Automated development tests do not claim installed-device acceptance.

## Vertical Atmospheric Profile

Ask: **How does air temperature change as my aircraft climbs or descends?**

1. Open **My Research → Vertical Atmospheric Profile → Fly Now**. Your plan includes the atmospheric instruments. **Customize** opens your own copy in Mission Planner, where you can change the question and sampling bands.
2. Click **Start mission**. Make sure science is **Collecting**; if it is armed, click **Start new science session**. Fly through different achievable altitudes in the simulator. Recording and science controls stay separate.
3. Click **End mission**, then **My Research → Review Atmospheric Profile → Evaluate Atmospheric Profile**. You should see actual temperature readings against altitude and which bands have enough data. Pressure, wind and density appear when available. **Inspect Reading** opens its recorded timeline. If something looks wrong, send a screenshot and say what you expected.

The default is three neighboring 500-foot MSL bands, with three consecutive valid temperature readings in each. There is no fixed starting altitude or required world location. Choose a sampling approach suitable for your simulated aircraft. **Completed collection** means sufficient recorded coverage; **Partial coverage** means some useful readings exist but requirements were not met; **Insufficient or unavailable evidence** means there are no valid temperature readings to establish coverage. These labels do not prove a hypothesis or certify an aircraft.

**Overview & Compare** compares selected flights using their own frozen definitions and original scientific units. Missing or changed evidence is not plotted as verified. Gaps and pauses are not interpolated, and unvisited bands stay missing. Chart/table previews are bounded; **Open Scientific Results** and **Open Recorded Flight Replay** retain the complete archive context. Atmospheric data is local to the aircraft and its recorded time: changing weather and horizontal travel can affect the profile. Simulator air temperature is different from historical satellite surface temperature.

Deleting collected science also removes its atmospheric summaries; flight recording alone cannot recreate them. This optional check can wait until your next installed Windows/MSFS test; automated development validation does not claim installed-device acceptance.

## Flight-Regime Validation

1. Open **My Research**. Under **Choose an Experiment**, find **Flight-Regime Validation**. Click **Fly Now** to prepare an example, or **Customize** to change your own copy in Mission Planner. Review the speed, altitude and hold time for your simulated aircraft; the examples do not suit every aircraft.
2. For a customized plan, click **Save mission plan**, then **Select for Flight**. In Mission Control, **Your Research Flight** explains what to do. Use the normal **Start mission** button, then **Activate Card**, **Begin Attempt** and **End Attempt**. Nothing starts automatically. **Deactivate Card** lets you switch targets without stopping flight or science recording.
3. Use **End mission** when finished. Open **My Research → Review Recorded Flight** or **Mission History → View mission**. In **Test-card Activity & Evidence Timeline**, click **Evaluate Attempt** or **Show Recorded Evidence**. Each attempt keeps its own card and timestamps. The original postflight UTC-interval evaluation is also available.

You can prepare a plan while disconnected. Connect MSFS and load an aircraft before recording. **Review Science and Storage Readiness** uses the existing advisory; normal startup checks still apply. This card needs flight telemetry, not an extra instrument or external dataset. Routes and other scientific instruments remain optional and keep their own requirements.

**Customize** keeps your original experiment and past results unchanged. Its **Your Experiment Targets** section edits a separate copy inside the full Mission Planner. You can still add routes, patterns, instruments or other cards. A failed save keeps your changes for retry. If you decline to replace an unsaved Planner draft, that draft stays in place. **Back to My Research** returns to your experiments. Saved cards also offer Fly Now and Customize; manual **Save Test Card** and **Open in Mission Planner** remain available.

**Pass** means the recorded speed and altitude stayed inside both tolerances for one continuous hold. **Fail** means enough evidence was recorded, but no hold met both targets. **Insufficient Evidence** means the recording cannot establish the result. Pauses, gaps and missing values do not count as a successful hold.

Speed is indicated airspeed in knots. Altitude is feet above mean sea level (MSL). Evidence times are UTC. Science-session controls remain separate. These simulator results do not certify an aircraft or physical instrument.

**Edit Card → Save New Revision** keeps old flights and results unchanged. Each flight uses the card revision selected before recording. Results preserve the flight, time range and original sample references. A result from demo telemetry is labeled as demo evidence.

## A short Windows check, when you next fly

- Open **My Research** and choose **Fly Now** under Flight-Regime Validation. You should see **Your Research Flight** with targets; recording should not start.
- Return with **Back to My Research**, then choose **Customize**. Change a target and click **Save mission plan**. Your other saved experiments should keep their old targets.
- If something looks wrong, stop that check and send a screenshot with what you expected.

No terminal commands or developer setup are part of this check. Automated tests cover the local workflow; an actual Windows/MSFS flight has not been claimed for this new feature.

## Understanding comparisons

Overview counts cover every retained flight for that experiment. **In Progress**, **Awaiting Evaluation** and **Interrupted** are different from a saved Pass, Fail or Insufficient Evidence result. Clicking Evaluate again preserves the evaluations without counting another marked attempt. Postflight intervals may overlap marked attempts; they are not independent trials.

Each comparison uses that attempt's original revision, targets and hold duration. A blue required-hold bar and a separately labeled recorded-hold bar have matching numbers in the accessible table. Missing, insufficient or changed evidence has no achieved-hold bar. A saved Pass from demo telemetry remains demo evidence. No statistical trend, aircraft certification or physical scientific-source acceptance is inferred.

**Refresh Experiment** clears the comparison selection and reloads current records. **Saved Local Results — All Evaluations** keeps earlier evaluations available. The work remains offline and sends no personal research to an organization.
