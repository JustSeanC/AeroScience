# Personal Research — Local Experiments

Your experiments stay on your computer. You do not need an account or internet access.

## Local Mission Operations

Open **Mission Operations** for your current research question, existing flight/session/card
controls, instrument/source status and the same mission map. **Focus Card** changes the
prominent objective without changing other active cards. After **End mission**, use
**Synchronized Evidence Timeline** to inspect original attempts, scientific readings and
pilot observations. The native toolbar places its compact controls on **Research**.
See the [short operations walkthrough and evidence limits](v0.7.0-mission-operations.md).
No organization account, internet connection or implicit sharing is required.

## Automatic Attempt Detection

Ask: **Can AeroScience keep my test attempts while I concentrate on flying?**

1. Prepare a custom or multi-point card and **Start mission** normally. In **Personal Research Test Cards**, click **Activate Card**, choose the **Test point** if shown, then **Enable Automatic Detection**. The native toolbar offers the same controls.
2. Fly toward the selected targets and hold them. Watch **Monitoring → Attempt Detected → Attempt Complete** and the recorded outcome. Fly clearly outside the targets before trying again. For an ordered card, choose the next point after its required repetitions finish and enable detection for that point. **Begin Attempt / End Attempt** still work; manual attempts take priority. These buttons never stop your flight or science collection.
3. After **End mission**, open **My Research** and the flight's results or **Overview & Compare**. Automatic and manual attempts stay separate, including Fail and Insufficient Evidence. Use **Inspect Custom Test Evidence**, **Review Multi-Point Flight** or the existing timeline/replay to see originals. If something looks wrong, send a screenshot and say what you expected.

Detection is off by default and only monitors the explicitly armed custom IAS/MSL point. It does not infer a pilot's intention from unrelated flying or operate on the six science starters. **Detected** is not **Passed**, **Point Completed** or **Whole Card Completed**. Unsupported/missing readings, pauses, disconnection, source/aircraft changes and gaps retain Insufficient Evidence protections. Simulator-only results do not certify an aircraft.

### Recognition policy and evidence

Policy **`quantitative-detection-1`** recognizes two consecutive supported readings within twice the selected tolerances. AND requires all selected near-target signals; OR requires the same supported branch across recognition. An attempt closes when the original evaluator establishes the required hold, after two supported readings beyond three times the tolerance, or after a bounded unsuccessful recognition interval of `max(5 seconds, twice the required hold)`. Enough measured evidence can establish Fail; a short or interrupted interval is Insufficient Evidence. Original target tolerances and continuous-hold/gap rules are unchanged.

Three supported readings outside the wider exit band are required before another automatic attempt. A long hold counts once, and toggling detection does not evade re-entry. Select only the point you intend to test; a recognized approach that meets this policy is retained even if unsuccessful. Changing to a different point is explicit. Ordered points cannot earn early credit, and repetitions need distinct original intervals. One automatic interpretation belongs to one point; manual Begin gives priority to the new manual window, using original sequence boundaries to avoid counting the same sample twice.

The existing recorder stores originals once. Background work reads at most 128 original frames for each of 16 eligible sessions per flight batch, with bounded event processing and finalization; the recorder never calls HDF/raster/attempt analysis. Constant-size checkpoints, frozen consent/card/point identities, original control events, original sequences/UTC/hashes and evaluator version are retained in existing stores. These work/page bounds do not cap retained attempts. Results use the existing charts, timeline, history, comparison and replay. Native messages retain their 8,000-byte bound.

Restart ends the interrupted flight and safely finalizes previously armed windows; it never resumes recording or silently re-arms cards. **Recover Automatic Attempts** after a flight replays only the original opted-in windows. Repeated scans retain the same finished IDs/results, verify original numeric evidence and detect missing/changed intervals. Finished attempts are not restamped under changed definitions. Recovery can run over several bounded background batches; reopen results after verification. No hidden telemetry/science archive is created, and missing/deleted originals cannot be reconstructed.

## Multi-Point Flight-Test Cards

Ask: **Can I complete a small, repeatable flight-test procedure?**

1. Open **My Research → Create Multi-Point Test Card**. Name your card. Edit the points, targets, **Continuous hold (seconds)** and **Required repetitions**. Use **Add Test Point**, **Remove Point** or **Move Point Earlier** as needed, then **Save Test Card**. **Guided Builder** and **Advanced Editor** keep the same complete settings.
2. Click **Open in Mission Planner**, save and select your mission, then **Start mission** normally. In **Personal Research Test Cards**, click **Activate Card**, choose a **Test point**, and click **Begin Attempt**. Hold its targets, then click **End Attempt**. Begin and end a separate attempt for every repetition. The native toolbar has the same point selection and controls. None stops your flight or scientific collection.
3. After **End mission**, open **My Research → Review Multi-Point Flight → Evaluate Multi-Point Flight**. Check each point's repetitions and outcome. **Inspect Original Point Measurements** and **Open Recorded Flight Replay** show retained evidence. **Overview & Compare** reviews repeat flights. If something looks wrong, send a screenshot and say what you expected.

The example **Operating Envelope Demonstration** has **Low-Speed Stability** (120 ± 5 knots IAS for 10 seconds), then **Altitude Demonstration** (3,000 ± 100 feet MSL for 10 seconds, twice). These are editable examples, not operating limits suitable for every aircraft. Both altitude repetitions need separate marked intervals. One 30-second hold still counts as one attempt.

There are 2–4 points, each with a stable identity, objective, IAS and/or MSL conditions, hold and 1–5 required repetitions. **AND** requires all selected conditions at the same recorded times. **OR** requires one selected signal to sustain its entire continuous hold; switching between IAS and MSL cannot join shorter holds into a pass. An unsupported OR branch stays Insufficient Evidence and is displayed separately from a measured failure, even when the other branch qualifies.

**Complete points in order** requires earlier points' repetitions to finish before a later qualifying attempt begins. Early attempts remain visible with **Not counted** and are never retroactively qualified. Without that option, choose points in any order. A completed repetition must be a distinct, nonoverlapping explicitly marked interval. Extra, failed and interrupted attempts remain in history. Changing a point or card never starts an attempt automatically.

The overall card is **Pass** only when every required point and repetition qualifies. Sufficient but nonmatching evidence gives an individual Fail; missing required values, insufficient coverage, pauses, gaps above 1.5 seconds, interruptions and changed source/aircraft retain Insufficient Evidence protections. Qualifying repetitions cannot mix aircraft or simulator/demo source identities. Completion is a simulator procedure result, not aircraft certification. Dashboard/native progress is provisional; postflight review recomputes from original evidence and verifies frozen requirements, marked identities, timestamps and hashes.

Advanced mode explains the original signal units, AND/OR policy, sequence/repetition semantics, source availability and revision. Unsupported future fields/versions remain read-only with their complete content preserved. A version-8 card contains a version-1 procedure composed of existing version-1 flight-test conditions; `flight-procedure-1` composes the existing Flight-Regime windows. Existing P10 one-point cards and all six starters keep their own versions and semantics. No raw recordings are copied, no new science database/recorder is added, and scientific collection/deletion ownership remains separate from the original flight telemetry.

Numeric condition bounds match P10. Individual marked attempts are bounded to two hours for synchronous control work: a longer attempt closes as Insufficient Evidence without scanning it or blocking flight/science. Postflight review is bounded to 24 hours; previews show 20 attempts with existing timeline pages for the remainder. These are processing/display bounds, not limits on retained research ownership. An additive point-review index is installed on existing mission-schema-10 archives without rewriting rows; science schema stays 4. Offline review rechecks original evidence; unavailable/deleted flight evidence cannot be reconstructed from hidden copies. P12 above adds opt-in automatic detection. Arbitrary groups/scripts and organization policy remain unimplemented.

## Create Your Own Flight-Test Card

Ask: **Can I hold my chosen airspeed, altitude, or both for one continuous interval?**

1. Open **My Research → Create Custom Test Card**. Give your experiment and test point a name. Choose **Require indicated airspeed (IAS)**, **Require altitude (MSL)**, or both. Set your targets, tolerances and **Required continuous hold (seconds)**, then **Save Test Card**. The 120-knot / 3,000-foot / 30-second values are examples; choose targets suitable for your simulated aircraft.
2. Click **Open in Mission Planner**, save your plan and select it for flight. Start recording normally. In **Personal Research Test Cards**, use **Activate Card → Begin Attempt**, fly your test, then **End Attempt**. Card controls also work in the native toolbar. They do not stop recording or scientific collection.
3. End the flight and evaluate the recorded attempt in **My Research**. **Inspect Custom Test Evidence** shows the original requirements, selected measurements, longest supported hold, missing evidence and original flight timeline. **Overview & Compare** reviews repeat attempts. If something looks wrong, send a screenshot and say what you expected.

**Guided Builder** and **Advanced Editor** edit the same definition. Switching views keeps your settings. Advanced mode explains the units, measurement sources, simultaneous-AND policy, missing-data behavior and **Definition & Revision Preview**. It is a form, not a code editor. A newer/unsupported definition stays read-only with its complete retained content; neither view may simplify it or save a downgrade.

When both conditions are selected, IAS and MSL altitude must be inside their inclusive tolerances at the same recorded times. When only one is selected, the other signal is not an evaluation requirement. One continuous hold is required; separate shorter holds cannot be added together. Original recorded timestamps, source/aircraft identity and selected values must be supported. The existing recorder still decides which original frames it can retain; this feature does not invent a missing partial frame or new simulator signal.

**Pass** means the selected conditions held long enough on supported original evidence. **Fail** means sufficient continuous evidence exists but did not meet the hold. **Insufficient Evidence** means the interval contains unsupported required readings, recording gaps over 1.5 seconds, pauses, changed source/aircraft, interruption or inadequate coverage. Even an earlier achieved segment cannot make an incomplete custom interval pass. This custom policy is versioned separately as `flight-regime-custom-1`; the existing Flight-Regime starter's historical policy is unchanged. The longest supported segment is still reported for diagnosis. No outcome certifies an aircraft or establishes flight safety.

Card version 7 adds an optional version-1 named point to the existing Flight-Regime definition. Saving edits creates a new immutable revision; a recorded flight keeps its frozen revision. Evidence review rechecks the original telemetry/event hash, frozen plan/card, outcome and hold. Plots contain bounded original dots rather than interpolated values; full evidence remains in the existing recording/timeline. No second database, recorder, raw archive, downloader or account is introduced. Custom IAS/MSL results depend on their original flight recording, not external scientific originals or a hidden collected-science copy. Existing flight deletion owns their evidence references; deleting collected science does not regenerate scientific observations from telemetry.

### Authoring extension boundary (P10 checkpoint)

This checkpoint supports exactly one named quantitative point, IAS and/or MSL, simultaneous AND and a required hold. Bounds are IAS 1–2,000 knots with positive tolerance below the target; MSL −2,000–100,000 feet with tolerance up to 10,000 feet; hold 1–3,600 seconds. These are supported parameter/work bounds, not an aircraft operating envelope or retained-history limit.

P10 did not implement multiple points, AND/OR groups, ordered sequences or repetitions; P11 above now supplies that bounded extension. P12 above adds bounded automatic detection; human-observation groups, aircraft eligibility and organization-published revisions remain future work. Unknown fields are retained on reads, while incompatible writes/preparations fail closed. Guided mode must only edit definitions it can represent fully and otherwise offer **Open in Advanced Editor** without deleting content. Organization policy must remain separate from local evaluation.

## Experimental Sensor Checkout

Ask: **How does my fictional temperature sensor respond to changing air temperature?**

1. Open **My Research → Experimental Sensor Checkout → Fly Now**. **Customize** opens your own model settings in Mission Planner. **Preview Synthetic Response** shows made-up example inputs only; it never records flight evidence.
2. Click **Start mission**, then **Start new science session**. In **Personal Research Test Cards**, click **Activate Card → Begin Attempt**. Fly through changing air temperatures, then click **End Attempt**. Recording and science keep running when you change cards.
3. Click **End mission**, then **My Research → Review Sensor Checkout → Evaluate Sensor Checkout**. Compare original input dots with the clearly labeled model-derived response. **Overview & Compare** reviews repeat flights; **Export Input References & Modeled Values** saves the verified bounded analysis. If something looks wrong, send a screenshot and say what you expected.

The only input instrument is the existing **Air Temperature** (`ambient-temperature`). It needs no internet, external source, organization or account. Only retained valid simulator-reported temperature observations in `degC`, with no calibration adjustment and with an original source identity, qualify. Position is optional for this model; missing coordinates are never invented. Raw flight context cannot fill missing scientific collection.

### Fictional model version 1

This is a mathematical thought experiment, **not real hardware, an extra MSFS instrument, or a measurement of detector accuracy**. It does not alter acquired temperatures or grades. `InstrumentProfiles` describes camera/scanner reference geometry; the existing scalar calibration system describes opt-in adjusted readings. Neither represents a time-dependent fictional response. P09 therefore adds one typed analysis over the existing original observation stream, not a new acquisition driver or science archive.

For sampled input `x` in °C, the target is `u = gain × x + offset`. Initialize a segment's output at its first target. At the next actual sampled input time, update:

```
y[i] = u[i-1] + (y[i-1] - u[i-1]) × exp(-(t[i] - t[i-1]) / responseSeconds)
```

The previous sampled target is held constant between readings as an explicit model assumption, not an assertion about unsampled air. One response time follows 63.2% of a sustained step. Gain scales Celsius readings; offset has Celsius units. Outputs are not clipped, rounded, or written back as original measurements. Version 1 has no random noise or latency.

Sampling selects the first usable original reading and then the first at least the configured interval later. Original timestamps remain unchanged. Invalid/unsupported or out-of-range input, duplicate/backward timestamps, dataset/source/session changes, pauses, connection/gap events and excessive gaps reset state and sampling. No response is continued across a reset. Preview reduction never changes the calculation or evidence digest. Charts show separate dots, without inventing a continuous curve through missing or thinned points.

Bounds: response time 0.1–120 s; gain 0.1–3; offset ±50 °C; ordered original-input limits within −150…150 °C (lower ≤100, upper ≥−100); sampling 1–30 s; gap 1–60 s and at least the sampling interval; 3–3,600 required modeled samples; 2–3,600 s supported duration; 0–100 °C required input span; 0.001–200 °C maximum absolute modeled output-to-current-target deviation. These bound a supported configuration, not retained Library/history capacity. Review windows remain at most 24 hours, previews at most 1,000 points, comparisons 2–8 attempts, using existing paged history.

**Pass** means the declared fictional-model tracking limit was met with sufficient original samples, duration and temperature variation in one unbroken interval near both requested boundaries. **Fail** means sufficient supported evidence exceeded that limit. **Partial Checkout** means some response evidence exists but coverage, variation or continuity is incomplete. **Insufficient Evidence** means there are not enough supported inputs to calculate a response. A constant-temperature flight may not exercise the default variation goal. None of these outcomes establishes actual detector accuracy. Starting collection after the beginning of a whole-flight review may leave that interval partial; use **Begin Attempt / End Attempt** during collecting, or the existing optional shorter UTC interval.

Frozen configuration/model versions, exact original input/dataset/session IDs, original times/units/source/quality, event breaks, plan identity and evidence SHA-256 remain attached to each attempt. Results are recomputed and verified from the original science archive; the saved attempt contains summary statistics and references, not another raw recording or hidden input cache. JSON export explicitly identifies modeled values, original references, settings, provenance, hashes and whether its preview is downsampled. Full original observations stay available in **Open Original Science Results**, and the existing timeline/replay provides flight context. Different settings, model versions, input sources and weather remain separate during comparison.

Permanent collected-science deletion removes dependent sensor results through the existing mission/science transaction and registered-backup purge. Unrelated raw flight recordings survive. Missing/deleted originals cannot be regenerated from those flight recordings. Installed Windows/MSFS and physical-device acceptance remain separate from automated synthetic/real-process tests.

## Scientific Observation Flight

Ask: **What can I describe and document during this flight?**

1. Open **My Research → Scientific Observation Flight → Fly Now**. **Customize** lets you change your own question, observation goal and optional instruments. No internet, downloaded data or organization is needed.
2. Click **Start mission**, then **Record Pilot Observation**. Choose a **Category**, write **Notes**, and click **Add observation marker**. **Save in-flight note** saves words without a location. In the native toolbar, open **Pilot Observation**, choose a category, write a short observation and click **Add observation marker**. Optional instruments need **Start new science session**; notes do not.
3. Click **End mission**, then **My Research → Review Scientific Observations → Evaluate Scientific Observations**. Look for your original words, times and available locations. **Inspect Observation** opens nearby recorded evidence. **Overview & Compare** compares repeated investigations. If something looks wrong, send a screenshot and say what you expected.

The default goal is three written records in the Observation, Finding or Note categories. A location marker without words does not meet that goal. **Collection Complete** means the documentation requirements were met. **Partial Collection** means useful documentation exists but requirements are missing. **Insufficient Evidence** means no usable documentation exists for the selected objective. These labels never prove that a written interpretation is true, and two notes are not automatically two independent scientific measurements.

Your original host UTC is recorded when you save; it is not an inferred simulator event time. A fresh recorded aircraft position may be attached to a marker. Missing locations stay unavailable, including while paused or disconnected, and are not filled from the flight path. You may require locations or every selected category in Customize. In-flight notes have no location; use a marker when that is needed.

Optional temperature, pressure, wind and density are separate original simulator readings with their own units and UTC. Near-note readings come from the preceding three seconds and do not prove what caused an observation. No reading is invented from a live display or raw flight context. If its Library is unavailable, the pilot documentation can still reopen while supporting values remain unverified. Preview tables/maps are bounded; the existing archive and timeline retain the original records.

Deleting collected science removes the associated Pilot Observations, research notes and dependent results, including registered app backups. Unrelated flight recordings and external source files remain separate. This optional three-step check can wait until your next Windows/MSFS flight; automated development tests do not claim installed or physical-device acceptance.

## Terrain & Temperature Survey

Ask: **What terrain and temperatures were sampled along my flight?**

1. Open **My Research → Terrain & Temperature Survey**. Keep **Air Temperature** for a flight needing no downloaded data, or select **Terrain Elevation** and/or **Land Surface Temperature**. Click **Fly Now**, or **Customize** to edit your own plan.
2. Click **Start mission**, then **Start new science session**. Fly to a few different places. For terrain or satellite temperature, **Review Selected Sources** and the normal Library/readiness controls explain what data is available. Missing data does not stop local air-temperature science.
3. Click **End mission**, then **My Research → Review Terrain & Temperature Survey → Evaluate Terrain & Temperature Survey**. Look for separate outcomes, units and sampled points for each measurement. Use **Overview & Compare** for repeated flights. If something looks wrong, send a screenshot and say what you expected.

The default needs three qualified samples at two locations at least 100 m apart for **each** selected measurement. **Collection Complete** means those collection goals were met; **Partial Collection** means some useful measurements exist; **Insufficient Evidence** means the selected goal has no qualified retained values. These are collection outcomes, not proof of a scientific hypothesis.

Terrain is DEM/DSM elevation in metres with its original vertical datum and grid resolution, **not airborne LiDAR**. Unknown datum stays unqualified; some USGS exports do not specify it. Landsat temperature is **historical satellite surface temperature in Kelvin**, with its original acquisition date and QA, **not an aircraft thermal camera**. Per-pixel uncertainty remains Unknown when the source did not retain it. Air temperature is simulator-reported Celsius at the aircraft. These are separate quantities.

Results show actual retained points, never a filled-in continuous scan. The flight line is context, not evidence of surrounding scientific coverage. Click **Inspect Saved Evidence** to follow a reading into the existing timeline. Datums/resolutions/source groups stay separate in comparisons. External-original deletion can leave independently saved observations available; deleting collected science removes dependent results and cannot regenerate them from the flight path. This optional three-step check can wait until your next Windows/MSFS flight; automated tests do not claim installed/device acceptance.

## Atmospheric Transect

Ask: **How do atmospheric conditions change as my aircraft travels across a region?**

1. Open **My Research → Atmospheric Transect → Fly Now**. **Customize** opens your own copy in Mission Planner. You can change the question, sampling locations and distance goals, or use the normal route tools. You can prepare free flight while disconnected.
2. Click **Start mission**, then **Start new science session**. Fly across different places in the simulator. Recording, science collection and card activation are separate. The default asks for three sampled locations at least 500 m apart, with three readings at each and 1,000 m extent in one supported leg.
3. Click **End mission**, then **My Research → Review Atmospheric Transect → Evaluate Atmospheric Transect**. Look for actual readings on the map and temperature chart. Click a sample or **Inspect Reading** to see its evidence. **Overview & Compare** lets you compare repeated flights. If something looks wrong, send a screenshot and say what you expected.

**Completed collection** means enough geographic science evidence was saved. **Partial coverage** means useful readings exist but the goals were not met. **Insufficient or unavailable evidence** means no valid located temperature science was retained. Stationary readings and repeat visits do not add new sampling locations. Pauses, time/position gaps and session changes break the sampled path; missing areas remain missing.

Distance includes supported links between recorded temperature samples, not unmeasured gaps. Lines illustrate sample order, not a continuous measured atmosphere. Altitude changes, elapsed time, weather and measurement settings can explain differences between places or flights. Pressure, wind and density appear only when supported original readings exist. Exact original coordinates remain in the table, including polar locations beyond the map's display range. Science deletion removes dependent transect results; raw flight recordings cannot recreate them.

These three optional app-only checks can wait until your next Windows/MSFS flight. Automated development tests do not claim installed-device acceptance.

## Vertical Atmospheric Profile

Ask: **How does air temperature change as my aircraft climbs or descends?**

1. Open **My Research → Vertical Atmospheric Profile → Fly Now**. Your plan includes the atmospheric instruments. **Customize** opens your own copy in Mission Planner, where you can change the question and sampling bands.
2. Click **Start mission**. Make sure science is **Collecting**; if it is armed, click **Start new science session**. Fly through different achievable altitudes in the simulator. Recording and science controls stay separate.
3. Click **End mission**, then **My Research → Review Atmospheric Profile → Evaluate Atmospheric Profile**. You should see actual temperature readings against altitude and which bands have enough data. Pressure, wind and density appear when available. **Inspect Reading** opens its recorded timeline. If something looks wrong, send a screenshot and say what you expected.

The default is three neighboring 500-foot MSL bands, with three consecutive valid temperature readings in each. There is no fixed starting altitude or required world location. Choose a sampling approach suitable for your simulated aircraft. **Completed collection** means sufficient recorded coverage; **Partial coverage** means some useful readings exist but requirements were not met; **Insufficient or unavailable evidence** means there are no valid temperature readings to establish coverage. These labels do not prove a hypothesis or certify an aircraft.

**Overview & Compare** compares selected flights using their own frozen definitions and original scientific units. Missing or changed evidence is not plotted as verified. Gaps and pauses are not interpolated, and unvisited bands stay missing. Chart/table previews are bounded; **Open Scientific Results** and **Open Recorded Flight Replay** retain the complete archive context. Atmospheric data is local to the aircraft and its recorded time: changing weather and horizontal travel can affect the profile. Simulator air temperature is different from historical satellite surface temperature.

Deleting collected science also removes its atmospheric summaries; flight recording alone cannot recreate them. This optional check can wait until your next installed Windows/MSFS test; automated development validation does not claim installed-device acceptance.

## Flight-Regime Validation

1. Open **My Research**. Under **Choose an Experiment**, find **Flight-Regime Validation**. Click **Fly Now** to prepare an example, or **Customize** to change your own copy in Mission Planner. Review the speed, altitude and hold time for your simulated aircraft; the examples do not suit every aircraft.
2. For a customized plan, click **Save mission plan**, then **Select for Flight**. In Mission Control, **Your Research Flight** explains what to do. Use the normal **Start mission** button, then **Activate Card**, **Begin Attempt** and **End Attempt**. Nothing starts automatically. **Deactivate Card** lets you switch targets without stopping flight or science recording.
3. Use **End mission** when finished. Open **My Research → Review Recorded Flight** or **Mission History → View mission**. In **Test-card Activity & Evidence Timeline**, click **Evaluate Attempt** or **Show Recorded Evidence**. Each attempt keeps its own card and timestamps. The original postflight UTC-interval evaluation is also available.

You can prepare a plan while disconnected. Connect MSFS and load an aircraft before recording. **Review Science and Storage Readiness** uses the existing advisory; normal startup checks still apply. This card needs flight telemetry, not an extra instrument or external dataset. Routes and other scientific instruments remain optional and keep their own requirements.

**Customize** keeps your original experiment and past results unchanged. Its **Your Experiment Targets** section edits a separate copy inside the full Mission Planner. You can still add routes, patterns, instruments or other cards. A failed save keeps your changes for retry. If you decline to replace an unsaved Planner draft, that draft stays in place. **Back to My Research** returns to your experiments. Saved cards also offer Fly Now and Customize; manual **Save Test Card** and **Open in Mission Planner** remain available.

**Pass** means the recorded speed and altitude stayed inside both tolerances for one continuous hold. **Fail** means enough evidence was recorded, but no hold met both targets. **Insufficient Evidence** means the recording cannot establish the result. Pauses, gaps and missing values do not count as a successful hold.

Speed is indicated airspeed in knots. Altitude is feet above mean sea level (MSL). Evidence times are UTC. Science-session controls remain separate. These simulator results do not certify an aircraft or physical instrument.

**Edit Card → Save New Revision** keeps old flights and results unchanged. Each flight uses the card revision selected before recording. Results preserve the flight, time range and original sample references. A result from demo telemetry is labeled as demo evidence.

## Understanding comparisons

Overview counts cover every retained flight for that experiment. **In Progress**, **Awaiting Evaluation** and **Interrupted** are different from a saved Pass, Fail or Insufficient Evidence result. Clicking Evaluate again preserves the evaluations without counting another marked attempt. Postflight intervals may overlap marked attempts; they are not independent trials.

Each comparison uses that attempt's original revision, targets and hold duration. A blue required-hold bar and a separately labeled recorded-hold bar have matching numbers in the accessible table. Missing, insufficient or changed evidence has no achieved-hold bar. A saved Pass from demo telemetry remains demo evidence. No statistical trend, aircraft certification or physical scientific-source acceptance is inferred.

**Refresh Experiment** clears the comparison selection and reloads current records. **Saved Local Results — All Evaluations** keeps earlier evaluations available. The work remains offline and sends no personal research to an organization.
